M3 The #1 rule

Firm. Flat. Level.

Elevate only on firm, flat, level ground. Tip-overs come from slopes, soft or broken ground, holes and drop-offs (hidden under water, ice, or mud), overloading, and wind. Stay within rated load. Outdoors, mind the wind limit in your manual (often ~28 mph [VERIFY WITH SME]). Guardrails up, gate closed — never climb or lean out to reach.

M4 Down low, still dangerous

Mind the danger zone

Operating from the ground? You're in the danger zone. Steer wheels can swing nearly 90°. Never stand between the lift and a wall or rack. Stay out of the drive path. Keep 360° awareness — use a spotter and barricades near people or equipment.

M4B Overhead & electrical

Look up — 10 feet from power

Look up before you go up. Power lines, beams, ductwork, sprinkler heads, and door frames all become strike or crush hazards when the platform rises.

10 ft Treat every overhead line as live. Keep the platform, your body, and tools at least 10 feet away from power lines — more for higher voltages [VERIFY WITH SME]. A scissor lift is not insulated.

M5 Key takeaway

Know both E-Stops

The red mushroom button is the Emergency Stop. There's one at the platform and one at the ground controls. Hit it the instant something goes wrong — it stops powered functions per that machine's design [VERIFY WITH SME]. Know where both are before you elevate.

M6 Key takeaway

Find your way down

Power dies while you're up? Use the manual descent — it lowers the platform slowly by gravity. Its location varies by machine (usually a valve or T-handle at the base or power module [VERIFY WITH SME]) — find it on the manufacturer's decal before you go up.

If you're stranded at height, this is a rescue procedure: a trained ground responder lowers you. Don't climb out. Every occupant should know the path down.

M7 Fall protection & exit

Rails up. Gate closed.

Guardrails and a closed gate are your fall protection on a scissor lift — keep them up, keep it closed. Three points of contact getting on and off. Don't exit at height unless the manufacturer allows it and a written procedure exists.

SITE RULE · EVERY ELEVATION

Hard hat on. Harness on and clipped to the platform anchor point.

Precision note: guardrails are the scissor-lift fall-protection method, and tie-off isn't federally required there — it is on boom-type lifts. The harness and hard hat are required here under site policy. Clip only to a manufacturer-rated anchor. [VERIFY WITH SME]

    CASE STUDY For portfolio reviewers

    What this module demonstrates

    Awareness ≠ Competency

    Teaches hazard recognition and never claims OSHA certification, operator authorization, or legal compliance — the awareness layer stays visibly separate from standard-specific competency.

    Behavior-based design

    The check tests decisions (Stop / Fix / Proceed) in realistic scenarios, not trivia — randomized 3-of-5 with corrective retries and no shaming.

    Dual coding

    Every rule is paired with a visual — isometric equipment, flat hazard scenes, numbered callouts, scenario illustrations — to cut cognitive load for multilingual, lower-literacy readers.

    Performance support

    The printable Pre-Use Card puts the job aid at the point of work — bridging Kirkpatrick Level 2 (learning) to Level 3 (behavior on the floor).

    Accessible & deployable

    Reduced-motion, full keyboard nav, a labeled radiogroup quiz, and a linear fallback. Single self-contained file, SCORM-ready for a real LMS.

    Compliance judgment

    Machine- and site-specific details are flagged [VERIFY WITH SME]; the mandatory harness/hard-hat site rule keeps the scissor-vs-boom tie-off distinction precise.
